# Collaboration brief for Prof. Minnie Sarwal or another transplant PI

## One-paragraph invitation

MOSAIC-Tx is an early, nonclinical research framework for prioritizing interventions against
patient-specific, multicellular kidney-allograft injury programs. We would value expert criticism
before real-data modeling—especially of cohort definitions, clinically credible failure modes,
assay choices, experimental validation, and whether the outputs answer a decision that matters.
The goal of an initial discussion is to revise or reject assumptions, not to request endorsement.

## Components where Prof. Sarwal's expertise could change the design

Public UCSF profiles describe relevant expertise in transplant immunobiology, single-cell and
single-nucleus RNA-seq, scATAC-seq, spatial imaging, TCR/BCR sequencing, proteomics/CyTOF,
biomarkers, drug repositioning, clinical trials, and commercialization. Concrete questions are:

1. Which ABMR subphenotypes must never be pooled, and which biopsy/treatment timing variables are
   essential to prevent clinically misleading signatures?
2. What constitutes a defensible stable-allograft reference, and which nonrejection injuries are
   the most important negative controls?
3. Which cell states and spatial relationships are sufficiently reproducible to anchor a model?
4. Which paired blood, urine, biopsy, antibody, protein, or clinical measurements are realistic for
   longitudinal validation?
5. Which readouts would make a blinded ex vivo experiment persuasive: endothelial activation,
   Fc/complement signaling, viability, histology, secreted proteins, or another functional assay?
6. Which safety constraints and concomitant immunosuppressant interactions should be hard vetoes?
7. What minimal result would justify a prospective observational study, and what result should stop
   development?
8. Which data-use, regulatory, IP, and translational requirements should be designed in now?

## Proposed first meeting

A 30-minute review of the one-page concept, the cohort diagram, and three deliberately challenging
synthetic cases. Desired output: corrected inclusion criteria, top three fatal flaws, one feasible
validation experiment, and a go/no-go decision for a fuller protocol. Authorship, data access,
funding, and IP would be discussed explicitly only if collaboration advances.
