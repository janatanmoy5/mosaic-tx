# Reference-aware qualification of immune and endothelial transcript programs in antibody-mediated kidney allograft rejection

The structured abstract is in [ABSTRACT.md](ABSTRACT.md).

## Introduction

Kidney transplantation improves survival and quality of life, yet long-term graft attrition remains constrained by alloimmune injury. ABMR is consequential because its manifestations are heterogeneous, treatment options remain limited, and microvascular injury can progress despite control of antibody or inflammation. A useful transcriptomic analysis must distinguish antibody exposure from damaging rejection and avoid treating every inflammatory signal as ABMR-specific.

DSA provides clinical context but is not sufficient evidence of active graft injury. DSA-positive grafts may remain non-rejecting, whereas cytotoxic, interferon, chemokine, and endothelial programs can also occur in TCMR. Separation of ABMR from non-rejection therefore does not establish separation from TCMR.

Randomized longitudinal data offer an orthogonal test. Felzartamab, an anti-CD38 antibody, was studied in late ABMR. The associated biopsy study reported suppression of NK-associated and interferon-gamma-inducible programs at week 24, diminished separation after treatment cessation, and less change in endothelial transcripts.

MOSAIC-Tx asks whether FCGR3A/Fc, NK/cytotoxic, interferon-gamma/chemokine, and endothelial programs (i) distinguish rejection from DSA exposure alone, (ii) replicate independently, (iii) remain distinct from TCMR, and (iv) change during randomized treatment. Discovery, replication, specificity, and response were tested separately.

## Methods

### Cohorts and estimands

This secondary analysis used public de-identified data. GSE50084 was the discovery cohort; GSE36059 was the replication and TCMR-control cohort; GSE275824 was the randomized longitudinal cohort. Matrices were not pooled across platforms. The replicate was a biopsy or patient.

GSE50084 primary inference used 61 kidney biopsies: 20 DSA-negative non-rejecting, 13 DSA-positive non-rejecting, and 28 DSA-positive rejecting. Its 54 blood profiles were audited but not pooled with tissue. GSE36059 included 281 non-rejecting, 65 ABMR, and 35 TCMR biopsies; 22 mixed-rejection and 8 nephrectomy profiles were excluded from primary contrasts. GSE275824 included 63 serial biopsies from 22 patients randomized 1:1 to felzartamab or placebo at baseline, week 24, and week 52.

The primary discovery estimand was DSA-positive rejection minus DSA-positive non-rejection. Supporting contrasts were rejection minus DSA-negative non-rejection and DSA-positive non-rejection minus DSA-negative non-rejection. Replication was ABMR minus non-rejection; specificity was ABMR minus TCMR. Longitudinal effects were (follow-up minus baseline in felzartamab) minus (follow-up minus baseline in placebo).

### Processing and analysis

scripts/build_public_data.py and scripts/build_bulk_cohorts.py compiled matrices and metadata. scripts/02b_annotate_bioc.R applied platform-specific Bioconductor mapping. Unmapped probes were removed and probes mapping to one gene were averaged, yielding 19,998 genes for GSE50084, 21,358 for GSE36059, and 19,434 for GSE275824.

scripts/03_bulk_de_gsea.R and scripts/04_external_validation.R fitted limma moderated models. scripts/05_treatment_response.R fitted longitudinal models using duplicateCorrelation for repeated biopsies. Genes were ranked by moderated statistic for Hallmark GSEA with fgsea.

Modules were prespecified in configs/mechanism_modules.json and scored by scripts/07_score_mechanism_modules.py. Each constituent gene was z-standardized across eligible biopsies within its cohort; the module score was the mean of available genes subject to minimum coverage. Scores are research quantities, not clinical units. Cross-sectional standardized differences and approximate 95% CIs were estimated by linear models. Longitudinal modules used patient-blocked treatment-by-time contrasts.

Deposited MMDx transcript-derived variables were analyzed without renaming or reconstruction. scripts/06_published_score_trajectories.R fitted random-intercept mixed models with patient as the random effect and arm, time, and arm-by-time as fixed effects.

Tests were two-sided. BH correction controlled FDR within each contrast family. Replication required same-direction FDR support in discovery and validation; replication against non-rejection was not called ABMR specificity without support versus TCMR.

PCA was descriptive. The 1,000 most variable genes were centered and scaled separately per platform. PCA did not enter hypothesis tests or classifiers. Figures were generated by scripts/15_make_dataset_result_figures.R and scripts/16_make_estimand_figure.R, with source CSVs exported. The complete verified workflow runs with bash scripts/run_three_dataset_pipeline.sh.

## Results

### DSA exposure was separated from rejection

The analysis included 505 prespecified kidney-biopsy profiles: 61 discovery, 381 validation, and 63 longitudinal biopsies (Figure 1A; Table 1). DSA-positive rejection versus DSA-positive non-rejection showed higher FCGR3A/Fc signaling (0.264, 95% CI 0.033-0.494; FDR=0.045), cytotoxicity (0.325, 0.103-0.546; FDR=0.020), interferon-gamma/chemokines (0.557, 0.195-0.920; FDR=0.020), NK identity (0.297, 0.093-0.501; FDR=0.020), and endothelial activation (0.428, 0.122-0.734; FDR=0.020) (Figures 1B and 2A). None differed between DSA-positive and DSA-negative non-rejecting biopsies after FDR correction.

Against DSA-negative controls, rejection effects were larger. At gene level, 1,468 genes met FDR<0.05, CXCL11 was a leading signal, and Hallmark allograft rejection was the leading enrichment.

### Replication did not establish specificity

All five programs replicated in GSE36059 ABMR versus non-rejection (Figures 1C-D and 2B): FCGR3A/Fc 0.423 (FDR=2.98x10^-5), cytotoxicity 0.508 (FDR=2.37x10^-6), interferon-gamma/chemokines 0.704 (FDR=9.86x10^-11), NK identity 0.539 (FDR=3.92x10^-8), and endothelial activation 0.496 (FDR=3.18x10^-10).

ABMR versus TCMR reversed interpretation for FCGR3A/Fc (-0.896; FDR=2.33x10^-6), cytotoxicity (-0.642; FDR=0.001), and interferon-gamma/chemokines (-0.767; FDR=2.34x10^-5), which were higher in TCMR. NK identity and endothelial activation did not distinguish ABMR from TCMR after FDR correction. These were rejection-associated, not ABMR-specific, programs.

### Treatment response was transient

At week 24, felzartamab relative to placebo reduced cytotoxicity (-0.915; FDR=0.025), interferon-gamma/chemokines (-1.122; FDR=0.009), and NK identity (-0.914; FDR=0.025) (Figure 2D). FCGR3A/Fc (-0.295; FDR=0.494), endothelial activation (-0.539; FDR=0.103), and tissue injury/repair (-0.046; FDR=0.892) were unsupported. No module remained significant at week 52 and no individual gene passed genome-wide FDR for either longitudinal contrast.

Deposited MMDx scores showed week-24 interactions for ABMR activity (beta=-0.798; FDR=0.005), ABMR interferon-gamma (beta=-0.866; FDR=0.013), and ABMR NK (beta=-0.522; FDR=0.019), but not ABMR endothelium (beta=-0.031; FDR=0.924) (Figure 1E-H). None was significant at week 52.

## Discussion

Four findings define this study. DSA exposure alone did not reproduce the coordinated state in rejecting grafts. That state replicated against non-rejection. TCMR falsified broad specificity claims for FCGR3A, cytotoxic, and interferon programs. Randomized data showed transient week-24 immune suppression, whereas endothelial activity changed less.

The DSA-conditioned result separates exposure from tissue response, but bulk expression cannot observe antibody binding or determine whether FCGR3A transcripts arise from NK cells, monocytes, or both. This is a mechanism-informed association, not a completed causal chain.

Shared inflammatory biology means that NK, FCGR3A, or interferon signals alone should not be presented as ABMR classifiers. Future biomarkers should combine DSA, histology, endothelial features, and immune programs and undergo disease-control and external validation.

The trajectories motivate a two-component model: dynamically suppressible NK/cytotoxic and interferon activity, and endothelial activation or injury that may resolve more slowly or require another intervention. Week-52 recurrence is compatible with loss of suppression, but limited power and biopsy variability remain alternatives.

Drug-reversal queries should use replicated, DSA-conditioned signatures rather than generic ABMR-versus-control lists containing shared rejection biology. Single-cell or spatial data may localize programs, but inference must remain donor-level. Docking, MD, or QSAR should follow only after a target and direction pass biological gates.

Limitations include heterogeneous platforms and diagnostic definitions, incomplete covariates, absence of clinical score thresholds, and the small longitudinal cohort. Bulk tissue cannot assign cell origin or causality; PCA is descriptive; and this analysis neither proves efficacy nor supports treatment decisions.
